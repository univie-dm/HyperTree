  Project Understanding

  Your project does Hyperbolic Hierarchical Clustering (HyphC) — learning
  tree-structured representations in Poincaré Ball space via triplet-based LCA
  losses. The pipeline is:

  1. Pretrain a ResNet backbone + hyperbolic projection with contrastive loss
  (SimCLR-style)
  2. Cluster by optimizing triplet LCA depths in hyperbolic space

  Currently for ImageNet, you can either:
  - Load supervised pretrained weights (load_supervised_weights: True) and
  freeze the encoder
  - Or pretrain from scratch with your contrastive loss

  ---
  Can you train ImageNet fully unsupervised?

  Yes, it's feasible but challenging. The core issue is that your contrastive
  pretraining needs to produce representations good enough that the clustering
  phase can discover meaningful hierarchies. Here's what would help:

  Augmentation improvements

  Your current augmentations are basic SimCLR-style. Stronger options:

  1. Multi-crop (SwAV/DINO style) — use 2 global crops (224px, scale 0.4–1.0) +
  several local crops (96px, scale 0.05–0.4). This gives much richer
  self-supervision for the same compute, and is one of the single biggest
  improvements in SSL
  2. RandAugment / AutoAugment — learned augmentation policies that go beyond
  the fixed color jitter + blur
  3. Stronger Gaussian blur — your current p=0.1 is quite low; SimCLR/BYOL use
  p=0.5–1.0 on one view
  4. Multi-scale RandomResizedCrop — your minimum scale is 0.2, but asymmetric
  crops (one tight, one loose) help learn scale invariance

  Architecture/method tricks (bigger impact than augmentations)

  5. Replace SimCLR-style contrastive with DINO/DINOv2 — self-distillation with
  a momentum teacher + centering avoids collapse without negatives and produces
  much better features for clustering. This is probably the single
  highest-impact change
  6. ViT backbone instead of ResNet — ViTs respond much better to SSL
  pretraining (DINO, MAE, iBOT). A ViT-S/16 pretrained with DINO from scratch on
   ImageNet gives features competitive with supervised ResNet50
  7. Momentum encoder (BYOL/MoCo v3) — you already have a two-view setup; adding
   an EMA teacher stabilizes training without needing large batch sizes
  8. MAE pretraining — Masked Autoencoder pretraining is very data-efficient on
  ImageNet and produces strong features. Could serve as a warmup before your
  contrastive phase
  9. Longer pretraining — SSL methods typically need 300–800 epochs on ImageNet
  to converge; your config has 1000 which is good, but with better methods you
  might converge faster

  Clustering phase tricks

  10. Prototype-based regularization — add learnable prototypes (like SwAV's
  Sinkhorn) during clustering to prevent degenerate solutions
  11. Gradual unfreezing — start with frozen backbone, then slowly unfreeze
  later layers to fine-tune for the hyperbolic structure
  12. Larger embedding dim — 32-dim might be tight for 1000-class ImageNet
  hierarchy; consider 64 or 128

  Practical recommendation

  The most impactful path, in order of effort vs. reward:

  1. DINO-style self-distillation + multi-crop — this alone closes most of the
  gap to supervised features
  2. ViT-S/16 backbone — pairs naturally with DINO
  3. Stronger augmentations (higher blur probability, asymmetric crops)

  Would you like me to look into implementing any of these — e.g., adding
  multi-crop support to your datamodule, or integrating a DINO-style pretraining
   loop?

● Why this makes sense for dendrogram purity

  The sweep now covers both sides of the pipeline:

  Pretraining side (feature quality → similarity quality):
  - Backbone: resnet50 has more capacity but resnet18 trains faster — worth
  checking if the gap matters with DINO
  - Contrastive weight 0.0 vs 0.5: Tests whether the hyperbolic contrastive loss
   during pretraining helps structure the embedding space for hierarchy, or
  whether pure DINO features are sufficient (backbone SNN handles the rest)

  Clustering side (directly affects hierarchy):
  - Temperature (0.2→0.1) vs (0.3→0.05): The sharper schedule pushes the LCA
  softmax to make harder decisions about which pairs are more closely related —
  more aggressive sharpening can produce cleaner hierarchies but risks
  collapsing subtrees
  - SNN k 10 vs 20: Larger k = smoother similarity landscape = potentially
  better global hierarchy but less fine-grained local structure. For ImageNet's
  deep WordNet hierarchy, the right k matters a lot
  - Latent dim: 64/128/256 — ImageNet has 1000 classes with ~15 levels of
  WordNet hierarchy, so higher dim gives more room to encode that depth

  The key insight: with use_backbone_knn_similarities: True and freeze_encoder: 
  True, the DINO backbone features are the fixed similarity signal that the
  clustering loss optimizes against. So DINO quality directly gates dendrogram
  purity — bad features → bad similarities → the clustering loss optimizes the
  wrong hierarchy.
